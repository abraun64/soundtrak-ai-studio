> **Audience note:** queued as a "B2B" award but the audience is prospective students — this is **B2C / higher-ed recruitment**. Filed accordingly; the authenticity-of-production craft is the transferable value.

## Strategic spine
Audience: high-schoolers weighing higher ed, especially STEM and space. Insight: prospective students want proof they'll belong somewhere that launches real careers. Big idea: position Purdue as the launchpad for the next generation of explorers via its "Cradle of Astronauts" legacy (27 alumni trained for space, incl. Neil Armstrong), with a deliberately diverse, inclusive cast.

## Creative move
**What it did**: a sub-10-minute short film shot on location at the Mars Desert Research Station with a physical shuttle built over six months, cast from real Purdue students and alumni.

**The move**:
- *Authentic production* — real sets, real students, no stock gloss.
- *Legacy + future* — 100-year heritage reframed for contemporary, diverse talent.
- *Platform arbitrage* — YouTube, underused by universities.
- *Cast for belonging* — diverse STEM protagonists so more prospects see themselves.

**Why it worked**:
- Authenticity is the credibility currency with Gen Z.
- Heritage lends stature; diverse casting widens the door.
- An underused channel wins disproportionate attention.

**When to steal**: a brand with genuine heritage; a young audience that punishes inauthenticity; a category defaulting to generic promo video.

**Anti-patterns / risks**: legacy nostalgia with no present-day relevance; "diversity" as set-dressing rather than substance.
