## Strategic spine
Dual audience: professional electricians (declining engagement) and consumers (safety-knowledge gap — 42% of Gen Z can't find their panel). Insight: the most important safety device in the building is completely invisible to the people it protects. Big idea: give the MCB a 100-year "lifelong protector" story so it becomes relatable and endearing to consumers while rebuilding pride and trust among the trade.

## Creative move
**What it did**: celebrated the MCB's centenary with humanising animation, AR exhibits and global contests.

**The move**:
- *Humanise the invisible* — anthropomorphise an unglamorous component via nostalgic animation.
- *Heritage as narrative* — a century-long legacy carries emotional and trust weight.
- *Dual-audience design* — one story serves consumer awareness AND trade pride.
- *AR + contest* convert passive heritage into active participation.

**Why it worked**:
- Emotional storytelling makes a spec-sheet product memorable.
- Legacy framing lends authority and warmth at once.
- Serving both audiences with one idea multiplies efficiency.

**When to steal**: a commoditised/invisible industrial product; a brand with real heritage; a category needing both trade and end-user attention.

**Anti-patterns / risks**: whimsy that undercuts the safety message; heritage nostalgia with no contemporary relevance.
